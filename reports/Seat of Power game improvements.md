# Make every decision visibly come back

The biggest single improvement available to Seat of Power is to **make the game say "because you…" every time something changes, and to show it on screen**. The game already stores far more cause and effect than players can see. Effects that arrive later (programme payoffs, revenue reforms, council-election fallout, debts coming due) change the meters at the start of a turn and leave only one line in the log ([JSX:11872-11878](game/seat-of-power.jsx)). The research is clear on what follows from that: people who cannot see the link between a past action and a later result blame outside events, even when they caused it themselves ([Sterman 1989](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:35:y:1989:i:3:p:321-339)), and players only feel in control when feedback names their specific choice ([Fendt et al. 2012](https://ciigar.csc.ncsu.edu/files/bib/Fendt2012-IllusionOfAgency.pdf)). The other three fronts tie into that one. Motion should carry consequences rather than decorate, because feedback that depends on what happened raised player motivation while simply louder feedback lowered it ([Kao et al., CHI 2024](https://researchr.org/publication/KaoBGBD24)). Clarity work should go to the five or so year-end and money screens that still carry 140 to 210 words and up to 23 numbers each. Navigation needs one "what needs me next" button that follows the player onto every tab, because mail and next steps currently live only on the Desk. Below are 16 ranked changes, none of which repeats what the game already has or brings back what was removed (the floating change readout, the motorcade, the in-year checklist, "How it really works" boxes). Most are a day to a week of work, and the whole list can be tested in four new on/off experiments rather than sixteen.

## Sixteen changes, ranked by payoff for the effort

The ranking weighs four things: how directly a change answers player complaints, how strong the outside evidence is, how much of the groundwork already exists in the game, and whether it suits the owner's history of cutting on-screen noise. Effort assumes one developer working with the existing code; "small" is about a day or two, "medium" about a week, "large" two weeks or more, mostly writing or art.

The top six share a pattern. Each one finishes a system the game already has, and none of them adds a new mechanic. That is deliberate. The audit found the game rich in delayed consequences, ceremony animation and onboarding aids. It is thin in the joins between them: the delayed path back to the player, the size of a change, and the route from wherever the player is to whatever needs them.

## Consequences: the game remembers, but rarely says so

### Rank 1: a "because you…" card for every delayed effect that matters

**What players see now.** Many choices come back later. Education programmes pay off three years on, health and farming two; revenue reforms are scheduled; finished programmes trigger a commissioner's letter and then an investor ([JSX:7675-7682, 9534-9536](game/seat-of-power.jsx); [STATUS:42-57](docs/STATUS.md)). When these land, the meters change quietly and one log line is written. Under the plain-words meters the player sees only an arrow, with no reason attached. The year-end lists outcomes without their origins. **This is the main gap in the game's cause and effect**, and it explains complaints like "the president is too angry for anything" and "how do I undo what was done": players feel punished because they cannot see what they did.

**What it would look like.** When a delayed effect of real size lands, it arrives as a short result card through the existing pop-up queue. A stamp in the corner reads "From your decision · Year 1" and the line says, for example, "The Ibadan Road clinics you funded in Year 2 are open. People ▲▲." The stamp opens the original letter. Small effects stay in the log, grouped into one "Also this half-year" card so they don't flood the queue. Outside events (oil price, FAAC shortfall, floods) get a different stamp, "Not your doing", so random bad luck stops reading as hidden punishment.

**The evidence.** Sterman's experiment found people poorly appreciate delays, misjudge how their decisions feed back, and blame outside events for swings they caused ([Sterman 1989](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:35:y:1989:i:3:p:321-339)). A widely used guide to meaningful choice lists reminders as a core requirement: "The player must be reminded of the choice they made after they made it" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/meaningful-choice-in-games-practical-guide-case-studies)). Crusader Kings 3's event chains show the effects already applied earlier in the same chain, so the callback carries its history ([Paradox forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/adultery-issues-in-the-stream.1411182)). Suzerain's developer describes the whole game as built on tracking and reporting back "most if not all decisions made" ([articy interview](https://www.articy.com/en/?p=4725)).

**Effort and groundwork.** Small to medium. Each delayed effect already stores a description; the record of decisions already stores the decision text and its effect on approval ([public/sop-ledger.js](public/sop-ledger.js)). The work is to carry the original decision's name and year with each delayed effect and send it to the existing result card instead of the log. A test should then check that every meter change above a set size carries a named cause, so nothing slips through silently.

### Rank 4: tap a meter to see why it moved

**What it would look like.** Tapping People, Party, Treasury or Godfather slides up a short sheet: "People · Warm · up a lot since last year", then three to five rows in plain words sorted by size ("Paid pensions on time ▲", "Teachers' pay 3 months late ▼▼", "Flood in Kogi, not your doing ▼"), then "Other". Each row opens the original letter or project. That is two levels deep and no more.

**The evidence.** Crusader Kings 3 lets players open an explanation inside an explanation, and its designer recommends stopping at two or three levels ([Philip Ardeljan](https://philip.design/blog/tooltips-in-tooltips/)). Civilization VI players complain that the game's breakdowns don't add up to the totals ([CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/why-dont-yields-add-up.631356)), and a popular fan-made add-on exists mainly to show the itemised causes of a city's mood ([CQUI on GitHub](https://github.com/civfanatics/CQUI_Community-Edition)). The lesson cuts both ways: **the rows must add up to the change**, so an honest "Other" row beats a list that silently misses things. Colour should mean good or bad for the player, not up or down. Democracy 3 players were confused when a policy pushing crime up showed green because the arrow tracked direction ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/245470/discussions/0/558747287687380501)). That matters here because a rising Godfather meter is bad news.

**Effort.** Medium, about a week. The before/after snapshots and the record of decisions supply most of the data; the work is tagging each change with its source.

### Rank 5: the year-end becomes "settling the accounts"

**What it would look like.** The heaviest screens in the game are the adviser's "How the last year went" (177 words, 20 numbers), "Year 2's money is in" (up to 166 words, 23 numbers) and "The year is done" (209 words) (round-2 audit, `screens.json` in the session scratchpad). Replace the adviser's prose and the five loose log lines with one fixed-order page: money in, money out, then people (the godfather, the House, the party, the public), each a short line with a face and an arrow. Below that, a simple stepped bar per meter shows where you started, the top three causes split into "your decisions" and "outside events", and where you ended. "📋 Full year review" stays for the curious.

**The evidence.** Papers, Please settles accounts at the end of every day in a fixed order: earnings, fines, then rent, food, heat and medicine. Its family screen was kept deliberately plain, names with tick-box states such as hungry or cold ([Papers, Please wiki](https://papersplease.fandom.com/wiki/Papers,_Please); [PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/183289-papers-please-morality-2495645423.html)). The chain from too many fines to an unheated flat to a sick child is easy to read ([Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/pc/Papers-Please-Review-374140.shtml)). Because of Sterman's finding, splitting "your doing" from "outside events" matters more than the chart itself.

**Effort.** Medium. Mostly layout and rewriting; the numbers already exist.

### Rank 12: mark the choices that will come back, and list what is coming due

**What it would look like.** The preview chips already show what a choice costs and moves now ([JSX:6377-6385](game/seat-of-power.jsx)), so the game does not need Reigns-style preview dots; it has the equivalent. What is missing is time. Add one small chip, "⏳ comes back next year", only to options with real delayed weight, and a one-line moment after choosing ("The Party Chairman will remember this"). A short "Coming due" list in the Desk shows promises and pending effects with a countdown. This extends the idea behind zone asks, which turned surprise penalties into visible requests ([STATUS:242-254](docs/STATUS.md)).

**The evidence.** Telltale added "X will remember that" because players forgot their choices mattered across weeks between episodes, and when the line was absent that also told players something ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-t-m-i-preserving-the-suspension-of-disbelief-through-ambiguity)). Democracy 4 shows effects still on their way as a faint "ghost" bar moving toward its final value ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2242836711)). Frostpunk 2 treats promises as stored debts that cut trust sharply when broken ([Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk-2/council/zf115fb)). The trade-off is real: critics say the Telltale line invites gaming the system, and a later Telltale game dropped it ([VG247 mirror](https://vg247ac.pages.dev/posts/telltale-will-remember-that-why-new-tales-from-the-borderlands-dropped-the-infamous-conversation-prompt)). Use the chip sparingly or it stops meaning anything.

## Motion: put the juice where the consequences are

The game has about 40 animations already, and they cluster at ceremonies: flights, signing and vetoing bills, newspapers, letters, achievements, coins for money in ([public/seat-of-power.html](public/seat-of-power.html), lines 162-322). Everyday changes get much less. The best evidence says to add motion there, at a moderate level, and only where it carries information. In the largest study of "juice" (3,018 players), versions with none or extreme juice did worse on play time and motivation than medium and high ([Kao 2020 summary](https://improbable.com/tag/rpg/)). A later study found feedback tied to the player's result raised every measured motive, while simply amplifying it lowered them ([Kao et al., CHI 2024](https://researchr.org/publication/KaoBGBD24)). One caution: both studies used action games. No controlled study of juice in card or strategy games was found.

### Rank 3: meter changes that show how big and how good

**The problem.** Under the plain-words meters, the change chip shows only ▲ or ▼, so a +1 and a +10 look the same ([STATUS:322-323](docs/STATUS.md)). This is where motion and cause and effect meet: the player cannot feel the weight of a decision.

**What it would look like.** One, two or three chevrons for small, medium and large changes, coloured by good or bad for the player. A rise gets a short green lift, a fall a short red drop. When a meter crosses into a new word (Warm to Restless, Healthy to Tight), that crossing gets its own brief beat, once, like Frostpunk's explicit warnings near critical levels ([Gamepressure](https://gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c)). The words stay; only the arrow gains size. Apple's guidance supports keeping a lasting mark after the motion ends, because motion should never be the only signal ([Apple HIG: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.md)).

**Effort.** Small, about a day. It changes the existing chip.

### Rank 10: a Compound map that shows what you did

**What it would look like.** Not background loops (the owner removed the motorcade and prefers less noise), but changes driven by the state: the protest overlay grows or clears as People moves; a project site shows scaffolding while a programme is running and a finished building once it opens; the press crowd gathers after a scandal; a flag rises on the mailbox when letters wait. After a decision that changed one of these, the camera lingers on it for half a second when the player returns to the map. This answers player asks for "actual scenes" and "let us walk and see how much is the state" without adding new screens.

**The evidence.** Frostpunk's meters are praised because they show consequences "right as they happen" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-review/)), and Cities: Skylines lets players see on the map where a problem comes from ([guidestrats](https://guidestrats.com/cities-skylines-how-to-make-citizens-happy/)). The warning from Frostpunk applies too: changes that matter little to how things run make the system feel hollow ([Slant](https://slantmagazine.com/games/frostpunk)), so every map change must reflect a real state.

**Effort.** Medium, mostly new overlay art from Lovable, which already supplies the painted map and has briefs for screens and scenes ([docs/](docs)).

### Rank 11: money going out deserves motion too

Coins rain only when money comes in ([HTML:670-684](public/seat-of-power.html)). Spending and FAAC-day leaks have no matching picture, so the game celebrates income and hides outflow, which may feed the "money is too hard" complaint (11 mentions in feedback). Add a short drain (a few coins sliding out of the treasury, the number counting down rather than jumping) for large payments and leaks. Numbers that count up or down instead of simply appearing are a standard reward technique ([PSU](https://www.psu.com/news/why-winning-feels-so-good-reward-design-from-arcades-to-balatro/)). Small effort, since it mirrors the existing coin code.

### Rank 15: housekeeping that protects cheap phones

Three small fixes belong in every version rather than an experiment. First, add a "Less animation" switch to the ☰ menu. The game honours the phone's reduced-motion setting, but it runs inside the Lagos Life app, and the research found no confirmation that such host apps pass that setting through (an unverified gap). Second, the meter bars currently animate their width; web performance guidance says to animate only movement and fading, which the phone's graphics chip handles even when the main processor is busy ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). That matters here: low-end phones made up 57% of the market in developing regions in 2019 data ([WebMedic, HotMobile '21](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~ravian/publications/webmedic_hotmobile21.pdf)), and real-user tests in South Africa showed devices slower than any standard lab phone ([Web Performance Calendar](https://calendar.perfplanet.com/2023/wikipedias-worldwide-web-cpu-benchmark/)). Third, the browser's built-in page-transition feature now works across major browsers ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available?hl=en)) and can slide between Desk, State and People with very little code, falling back to an instant swap on older phones. Keep everyday motion short, about a quarter of a second, and never make the player wait for it; Apple advises against motion on frequent actions ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.md)).

### Rank 16: the flying token, as a trial only

The pattern most cited for linking cause to effect is a small token (a coin, a party flag, the godfather's ring) that flies from the chosen card to the meter it changes, which then pulses ([Game Developer: Empires & Allies](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/clicking-and-rewards-in-empires-and-allies)). It ranks last because it sits close to the floating change readout the owner removed at a player's request ([STATUS:85](docs/STATUS.md)). If tried at all, it should be off by default, limited to large changes and the first few times, and judged against Rank 3, which may make it unnecessary.

## Clarity: shorter where it is still long, and help that answers "what is this screen?"

The rebuild already cut the governing screen from 306 to 151 words and decision screens by about half ([STATUS:133-135](docs/STATUS.md)). Yet "confusing / too much English" remains the top feedback theme with 15 mentions, with quotes like "pls the english is to much" and "so many things happening at once". Two cautions apply: the feedback runs only to 5 November, around the round-2 rebuild, and the experiment results that would show whether confusion dropped live on the server dashboard, not in these notes. Still, the evidence says guidance pays off in a game this complex. Across 45,000 players, tutorials raised play time by up to 29% in the most complex game tested and had negligible effect in simpler ones ([Andersen et al., CHI 2012](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/)). Text-only screens also fail many first-time and lower-literacy users, who strongly prefer pictures and voice ([Medhi et al.](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). That matters in Nigeria, where measured youth literacy ranges from 49% to 81% depending on the method ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=443095); [MeasuredWorld/UNESCO](https://measuredworld.com/countries/nigeria/education)).

### Rank 7: a plain-words sweep of the words that survived

"Godfather" sits on nearly every governing screen and is a meter name, yet it is not in the plain-word swap list. "FAAC", "bill", "tribunal", "INEC" and "EFCC" also persist, and capitalised runs like "OGUN, STATE, REF, GH" appear on many screens (round-2 audit). The fix: the first time any term appears, it carries its meaning in brackets in the sentence itself ("FAAC money (our monthly share from Abuja)"), in 12 words or fewer, with the tap-glossary kept as the second layer. The first underlined word should open its meaning once by itself, so players learn that underlines can be tapped; nothing in the game tells them today. Keep one term per idea everywhere (budget, never also "appropriation"). Sentences of about 14 words are understood by more than 90% of readers ([Siteimprove, citing American Press Institute](https://help.siteimprove.com/support/solutions/articles/80000447968)), and repeating nouns instead of pronouns helps second-language readers ([King County](https://kingcounty.gov/help/editorial-style-guide/plainwriting/simplesentences.aspx)). A simple picture map of the six geopolitical zones, which one player had to look up online, belongs in the same sweep. Small effort.

### Rank 9: the Hint button explains this screen, then steps back

The 💡 Hint already exists in the first two years and offers "Take me there" ([pillars.js:76-83](game/systems/pillars.js)). Extend it so that on any sub-page it answers "what is this screen and what do I do here?" in two lines from the Chief of Staff, with a face. After year 2, switch it to warnings only: it speaks up when something is about to go wrong. Civilization VII does exactly this, with tips "exactly when you first encounter something new" and an "Only Warnings" setting once players know the basics ([Game8](https://game8.co/articles/latest/civilization-7-tutorial-recommended-for-veterans-and-newcomers-alike); [Civ VII guide](https://civilization.2k.com/en-GB/civ-vii/news/civilization-vii-beginners-guide/)). On the first visit to each sub-page, dim everything except the one control to use, with one sentence, shown once. Small to medium effort, mostly writing about 15 two-line explanations.

### Rank 14: Pidgin where the reading is heaviest

Pidgin, Yorùbá, Hausa and Igbo now cover the cold open, hints, tabs, the four meters, buttons and headings, while everything else stays in English ([STATUS:225-227](docs/STATUS.md)). The heaviest English is in exactly the places players most need to understand: the adviser's annual letter, the money screens and result cards. Translate those next, starting with Pidgin, written the way Nigerians write online rather than formally. Pidgin is a second language for many Nigerians and for some has pushed English to third ([WAJOFEL](https://wajofel.org/index.php/wajofel/article/download/327/253/951)), and Nigerian apps already localise this way ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2577811)). The adviser can deliver definitions in-world ("Oga, FAAC money don land"). Large effort, mostly writing, but Ranks 5 and 7 shrink the text first, so less needs translating.

## Navigation: one button that always knows what needs you

The game has three bottom tabs, which fits the guidance of three to five top-level places ([Material Design](https://material.io/components/bottom-navigation)). The trouble is underneath: State has four sub-pages and People five, shown as a sideways-scrolling row of pills on phones, so the later ones are effectively hidden. On top of that come full-screen decision sheets, a queue of pop-ups, a ☰ menu of about 12 items, and the mailbox, which appears only on the Desk ([JSX:12525-12529, 12811](game/seat-of-power.jsx)). Players say "it's hard to navigate" and "I keep seeing 9 mails but no indication where to read them". Hidden navigation is used less: on phones, people used hidden menus in 57% of cases against 86% for partly visible ones ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-mobile-even-hamburger/)).

### Rank 2: one "Next" button, and the mail, on every tab

**What it would look like.** The game already picks the one next task ("Pass this year's budget", "Open your mail…") and can route the player to it ([JSX:12653, 12660](game/seat-of-power.jsx)). Today that button sits above the hub map. Move it into a single pill in the bottom-right thumb area, on every tab, merged with the mail count: "✉ 3 · Next: answer the Commissioner". Tapping it opens the exact sheet. When nothing waits, it reads "End the half-year". It must never get stuck and must always allow skipping.

**The evidence.** Civilization VI's End Turn button doubles as a queue of what needs you and jumps to the next item; its known failures are getting stuck and changing meaning without warning ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/289070/discussions/0/312265327165650851)). Clash Royale keeps its navigation always visible and low on the screen for one-handed play ([GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/articles/what-can-online-apps-learn-from-clash-royales-user-interface)), and observation of 1,333 people found about 75% of touches come from a thumb ([UXmatters, Hoober](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php?)). Football Manager 27 is being rebuilt specifically to stop players hunting between an inbox and separate screens to act on a message ([FM27](https://www.footballmanager.com/fm27/features/fm27-clearer-interface-smoother-navigation)).

**Effort.** Small to medium. The task picker and mailbox exist; this moves and joins them.

### Rank 6: make phone Back reliable on every layer

The current Back handler closes the top layer by searching for a button labelled Back, Close, Cancel and similar ([HTML:905-940](public/seat-of-power.html)). Result cards use "Okay" and "Got it", which it does not match, so Back can fail exactly on the most common pop-up. The fix is for each layer to register its own close action when it opens, so Back always closes the top layer, then returns to the tab's front page, then to the Desk, and at the Desk asks "Leave the game?". That follows Android's own rule that Back steps through a predictable stack ending at one fixed start screen ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/principles)). Because the game runs inside Lagos Life, the host app owns the hardware Back button, and browser tricks can work in testing yet fail on the device ([Ionic forum](https://forum.ionicframework.com/t/ionic-4-pwa-back-button-not-closing-modal/148663)), so test on a real Android phone inside the app. Small effort, and it is a bug fix: ship it to everyone.

### Rank 8: tab front pages with labelled rows

Replace the sideways pills on State and People with a short front page: one row per sub-page, each with a one-line status ("Treasury · ₦4.2bn · 2 leaks", "Cabinet · 1 seat empty"). Every sub-page gets a title like "State › Treasury" so the player knows where they are. Hold a depth limit: tab, sub-page, sheet, and never a sheet on top of a sheet. Give the Compound buildings the same names and icons as the sub-pages they open, so the map and the tabs teach one mental map instead of two routes. Badges appear only for things the player can act on now, red for a deadline, a plain dot for something new to read, and clear when opened; a dot that never clears stops meaning anything ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/beware-red-dot-badging.md)). Medium effort. Football Manager's backlash after redesigning navigation all at once is a warning to change this in steps and test each one ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/news/9-things-football-manager-26-needs-to-fix-immediately-to-get-back-on-side)).

### Rank 13: every mention of a place opens that place

Wherever the game names a place, the name should take you there with the item highlighted, and Back should return you to where you were: adviser lines, "Next:" lines on result cards, rows in the "why it moved" sheet, the year-end page. The game already does this for hub tasks through "Take me there" and "Show me"; this extends it everywhere. Android's guidance is that such links should build a realistic back path, as if the player had walked there ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/principles)). This answers "no clear instructions" by showing the place instead of describing it. Medium effort.

## Testing: four bundled experiments instead of sixteen small ones

The game already splits players at random into on/off groups ("pillars"), switchable with `?exp=`, and the guidance is to trust a difference only once both groups have 100 or more players ([STATUS:187-192, 309-311](docs/STATUS.md)). With 11 pillars already live, testing 16 changes one at a time would take far too long. Bundle them into four new pillars, one per front, and ship the pure fixes (Back, the animation switch, lighter meter bars) to everyone.

| New pillar | Contains (ranks) | Main measure | Supporting signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `why` | 1, 4, 5, 12 | Reached an ending; share who start year 3 | Fewer "punished / undo" comments at the year-2 feedback prompt |
| `nav` | 2, 8, 13 | Left in under 5 minutes; time from mail arriving to mail opened | Fewer "hard to navigate / where is my mail" comments |
| `juice` | 3, 10, 11 (16 as a sub-test) | Session length; half-years played per session | No rise in "too much going on"; frame rate on a low-end phone |
| `plain` | 7, 9, 14 | Left in under 5 minutes; reached end of year 1 | Taps on the Hint; fewer "confusing / English" comments |

Two measurement additions would make every test sharper. First, log each early step separately (first decision, first letter, budget passed, end of year 1), because players tend to drop off at single steps rather than steadily ([Unity](https://blog.unity.com/technology/standard-events-explained-part-1-onboarding)); a cliff at one step tells you exactly which screen to fix. Second, track next-day return. Median next-day retention for mobile games was about 22% in early 2025, with the top quarter just over 30% ([GameDevReports on GameAnalytics](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)), which gives Seat of Power an outside yardstick. Finally, for the "why" features, add an automatic check that every preview chip matches the result that follows. Players stop trusting a forecast that is ever wrong; one design principle puts it bluntly: "Telegraphs that lie or omit are worse than no telegraph" ([game-mechanics wiki](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/concepts/enemy-intent-telegraph)).

Some limits are worth stating. Most outside sources were read through search summaries because the original pages were blocked, so exact study figures were not checked against the full text. The experiment results and feedback after 5 November were not available, so some confusion may already be fixed. And no study tested these patterns in a political card game played on Nigerian phones; the experiments above are how to find out.

## Conclusion

The four fronts turn out to be one problem seen from four sides. Players call the game wordy partly because words are doing the job that pictures of cause and effect should do. They find it hard to navigate partly because the thing that needs them is on a different tab from where they stand. And motion feels thin not because there is too little of it, but because it celebrates ceremonies rather than consequences. So the bold move is not a new system or a redesign. It is to make the game's existing memory, its record of decisions and its queue of delayed effects, the voice of the interface, so that every change on screen arrives with its reason and a door back to the choice that caused it.

That changes what to build first. Rank 1 and Rank 3 together give every decision a visible size and a visible return, for a few days' work, and they make the later items cheaper: the "why it moved" sheet, the year-end settlement and the place-links all reuse the same "this came from that" tagging. If the `why` pillar moves completion rates, it is evidence that "confusing" in this game partly meant "I can't see what my choices did", and that the next round of clarity work should explain consequences rather than cut more words.
