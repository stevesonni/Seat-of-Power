# Brief: brainstorm the Senator, LGA Chairman and Ambassador careers

*Paste everything below the line into Google AI Studio or Gemini.*

---

You are helping design a mobile web game, **The Seat of Power**, a Nigerian politics simulator played mostly by young Nigerians on their phones (Lagos and the South-West most of all). The main career is the **Governor** (8 years, letters on a Desk, ministries, projects, the House of Assembly, godfathers, strikes, courts), with the **President** on top. Three smaller careers sit beside it: **Senator**, **LGA Chairman** and **Ambassador**. I want ideas to make these three feel like *lives* rather than short card games.

**Rules for your ideas**
- Every person and party is fictional. Real institutions and unions are fine (INEC, EFCC, NLC, ASUU, the Senate). No real politicians and no real logos or seals.
- The Ambassador's host countries are invented (no real countries, flags or cities).
- Each choice must change something real later (a number, a person's attitude, a future event). No "tap and see" flavour text.
- Teach how Nigerian government actually works (the Constitution, real laws), in one or two plain sentences at a time.
- Mobile-first: a few taps a turn, short text, no long reading.
- Don't sell a better result. Players buy *more play* (the rest of the term), never power.

**What players have told us:** they love that it "makes me appreciate what those in power feel". They want more to do each year, a career ladder ("start at local government and end at presidency"), Senate and House of Reps voting, and clearer explanations.

## 1. The Senator (4 years: 8 sessions, two a year)

**Stats (0–100 unless noted):** Home support (decides re-election), Clout (standing in Abuja: what your vote can buy), Party loyalty (decides whether you get the ticket again), Corruption (0–1), War chest (₦ billions).

**How you start:**
- **Story mode:** you come up from a finished governorship, and your record becomes your stats.
- **Free play:** you pick one of three starts:

| Start | Home | Clout | Loyalty | Corruption | Chest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Former governor | 52 | 60 | 60 | .30 | ₦2.5B |
| Popular activist | 60 | 30 | 45 | .08 | ₦0.4B |
| Godfather's candidate (he wants returns) | 45 | 50 | 70 | .25 | ₦4B |

**Session loop:**
- **The race for the seat:** 4 weeks, one move each week:
  - rallies;
  - visiting traditional rulers;
  - running on your record;
  - radio in every language;
  - cash on election eve (works, but it's a crime).
- **The Senate President election** (week one): back the President's choice, a rebel from your party, or the opposition's man. If your man wins, you get a committee: Appropriations, Public Accounts or Petroleum.
- **Two bills each session:**
  - one from the President, plus one from the floor;
  - the Appropriation Bill every second session.
  - You vote yes, no, or **trade** your yes for a federal project in your district (this needs clout).
  - Your district judges *your vote*, not the result.
  - Bills the President didn't want may pass and then be vetoed. You can lead a two-thirds override (73 of 109 votes).
  - 14 bills, e.g. fuel deregulation, state police, external loan approval, grazing routes, the Electoral Act, presidential immunity, social media regulation, LG autonomy, minimum wage.
- **Oversight hearings:** a minister in the chair and something rotten in the books (e.g. ₦38B for transformers never delivered). Grill them on live TV, send a report to the EFCC, or take an "appreciation" envelope (25% risk of exposure). 6 hearings.
- **The constituency fund:** ₦1B a year for boreholes, health posts, rural roads, scholarships and transformers. You can quietly "manage" up to the leftover, with a risk of exposure.
- **Your own bill, once a year:** 5 bills, e.g. a Federal University for your district. The chance it passes depends on your clout.
- **Events, one a session:** 7 in all:
  - the other party wants you to defect;
  - a lobbyist with a briefcase;
  - bandits attack your district;
  - budget padding (your share is ₦300M);
  - the ASUU strike;
  - your sponsor calls in his debt;
  - ministerial screening (a fake certificate; "take a bow and go").
- **Drift between sessions:** Home −7, Clout −3 ("what have you done lately?").

**The end:**
- **Re-election:** the party denies the ticket if Loyalty is under 35. Otherwise your vote share comes from Home, your war chest and your Corruption.
- **Score:** Home 35%, Clout 20%, delivery and laws 25%, clean hands 20%. Graded A–F.
- **Notebook:** 15 "how it really works" notes (Sections 48, 50, 58, 68, 69, 88–89, 147…).
- **Price:** the first year is free; the full term costs ₦1,000.

**Weaknesses today:**
- There are no named recurring characters, and choices don't come back later.
- Each session is the same shape.
- There is no House of Representatives.
- Committee work is thin.
- There's no rival senator.
- Everything happens to you; nothing comes from your own initiative.

## 2. The LGA Chairman (3 years: 6 half-years)

**Stats:** People (support at home), Governor (his favour), Council (the councillors' support), Corruption (0–1), Purse (₦ millions in the council account).

**How you got the ticket:**

| Start | People | Governor | Council | Corruption | Purse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The governor's choice | 45 | 72 | 70 | .20 | ₦60M |
| The people's favourite | 66 | 42 | 50 | .05 | ₦20M |
| The wealthy returnee | 52 | 50 | 45 | .08 | ₦120M |

**Half-year loop:**
- **The allocation:** ₦520M comes down through the state each half-year. Salaries take ₦260M first. The governor deducts 10–55% depending on his favour. You accept quietly, demand it back, or sue (the 2024 Supreme Court ruling on LG autonomy).
- **The in-tray:** you handle **two matters yourself**. The rest go to staff, who do the cheapest thing, and a neglected problem comes back worse.
- **Problems with faces (7):**
  - no nurse at the Ward 4 health centre;
  - the market road is cut;
  - a market fire;
  - two villages fighting over farmland;
  - children with no birth certificates;
  - the refuse heap;
  - primary teachers on strike.
- **Council motions (5):**
  - councillors want a "constituency allowance";
  - a bye-law to ban okadas;
  - double the market levy;
  - the party wants jobs for members;
  - impeachment whispers.
- **The governor's asks (3):**
  - fill buses for his rally;
  - sign council land to his friend;
  - give the drainage contract to his man.
- **The market road:** one project you live with all term.
  - You choose the builder: the party's contractor, an open tender, or three small roads.
  - A named trader warns you about a problem, and you fix it, inspect it, or push on.
  - The rainy season then tests the road's quality.
- **Choices come back:** 12 callbacks with named people. For example:
  - the land you gave away gets the traders evicted;
  - the crony's drains collapse in the first rain;
  - the clinic you staffed holds when cholera comes;
  - the market women you helped endorse you.
- **Named people:** the governor, the Party Chairman (who holds the ticket) and a Councillor (who can lead an impeachment). Each remembers you.
- **One promise on day one:**
  - the road will last;
  - no mother dies for want of a nurse;
  - or no new levies on traders.
- **Threats:**
  - a governor you defied can dissolve the council;
  - councillors you ignored can impeach you;
  - People drift −5 each half-year.

**The end:**
- **Council election:** run by the state commission (SIEC). The party chairman decides the ticket.
- **Verdict:** Delivery, Integrity, Survival and Your Promise, plus a grade.
- **Notebook:** 15 notes (774 LGAs, the Fourth Schedule, the joint account, SIEC, caretaker committees…).
- **Price:** the first year is free; the full term costs ₦500.

**Weaknesses today:**
- There is only one big project.
- Ward-level life isn't really shown (councillors are one person).
- There's no revenue side beyond levies.
- Traditional rulers appear only in disputes.
- There's no path upward (from Chairman to House of Assembly, Rep, Senator or Governor).

## 3. The Ambassador (3 years: 6 half-years)

**Stats:** Abuja (the President's favour), Host (the host government), Diaspora (Nigerians abroad), Trade (deals won, counted), Corruption (0–1).

**Postings (invented countries):**
- the Republic of Valdoria: 12,000 Nigerian students, strict police, buys Nigerian gas;
- the Kingdom of Ostrava: Nigerian traders, tough immigration, a king who loves ceremony;
- the Federation of Meridia: rich and eager to invest.

**Who you are:**
- a Career diplomat: Abuja barely knows you;
- a Political appointee: the President takes your calls, but the host isn't impressed;
- a Diaspora champion: the community trusts you, Abuja doesn't.

**Half-year loop:** one item from each of three lists.
- **A cable from Abuja (6):**
  - win their vote for our UN seat;
  - sign the trade deal by Friday;
  - deny the forged-visa story;
  - ask them for a loan;
  - the President is coming with 40 people;
  - the mission's budget is cut by a third.
- **A citizen's case (6):**
  - a mechanic held without charge;
  - students stranded by unpaid scholarships;
  - traders' containers seized;
  - the passport queue round the block;
  - riots, with Nigerians attacked;
  - a Nigerian on death row.
- **The host or the community (6):**
  - the host minister's complaint about online fraud (visa threats);
  - a trade fair;
  - a diaspora town hall;
  - the Independence Day reception;
  - a factory investor;
  - the Super Eagles playing there.
- **Drift:** each half-year Diaspora −6, Abuja −4, Host −2.

**The end:**
- **Threats:** a low Abuja score gets you recalled; a low Host score gets you expelled (persona non grata).
- **Score:** Diaspora 35%, Abuja 20%, Host 20%, Trade 15%, clean hands 10%.
- **Ending:** a strong score with Abuja onside means promotion; otherwise the posting simply ends.
- **Notebook:** 14 notes (Section 171, letters of credence, the Vienna Conventions, NiDCOM, remittances…).
- **Price:** the first year is free; the full posting costs ₦1,000.

**Weaknesses today:**
- It's the thinnest of the three: 18 items in all, so one posting sees almost everything.
- Choices don't come back later, and there are no named recurring characters (a Foreign Minister, the Host Minister and a Diaspora Leader are planned).
- There's no evacuation chain and no single big diplomatic crisis arc.
- The host country has no politics of its own.

## What I want from you

1. For **each** career, a **core tension** in one line, and the **one resource** that forces trade-offs.
2. **Three named recurring characters** per career, with what each wants and how each can help or hurt you.
3. **10 decision cards** per career that **come back later**. Give each the setup, 2–3 options, and what happens one or two turns later because of the choice.
4. **One big arc** per career that runs the whole term, like the LGA market road. For example: the Senator's own bill fought through committee, or an evacuation crisis for the Ambassador.
5. **The career ladder:** how Chairman → House of Assembly → House of Reps → Senator / Governor → President could link up. Say what carries over (record, enemies, money, a godfather).
6. **The House of Representatives**, which players asked for: is it a separate career, or merged with the Senate?
7. **One real-law fact** per card, in a sentence a 16-year-old understands.
8. Keep each card under 40 words, with options under 8 words.
