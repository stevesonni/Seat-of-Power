# Lovable art: sport and stadiums

Six pictures for the new sport stories (the Youth Assembly, the mini-stadia, FIFA and CAF, and the state's club in Africa). The game already checks for each file: until it arrives, it uses a stand-in, and when you drop the file in, the game uses it by itself.

## Rules (as docs/LOVABLE-DECISIONS.md)
- **Style:** painted, warm and semi-realistic, the same as the other cards. Not 3D, not photographic.
- **Setting:** Nigeria as it really looks.
- **What must not appear:**
  - real people, real players or real officials;
  - readable words, logos, club crests, FIFA or CAF marks, sponsors, or party symbols. Kits are plain colours with no badges or numbers that spell anything;
  - coats of arms or flags with a crest. A plain green-white-green flag is fine.
- **Mood:** honest. Pride where it is earned, decay where it is real. Nobody hurt.
- **Scenes:** 1600×900 (16:9), WebP (or PNG), no transparency. Keep the subject in the middle 60%, because phones crop the edges.
- **The portrait:** 1024×1280 (4:5), transparent background preferred, head to waist, the same as the other character art.

## The pictures

### 1. `public/art/scenes/stadium-derelict.webp`: a national stadium left to rot
*A huge old concrete stadium in a Nigerian city, decades past its best: the running track cracked and overgrown, weeds and a few goats on the pitch, rusted floodlight towers, broken plastic seats, a faded scoreboard with no words, traders' stalls squatting under the stands. Late afternoon light. It was once grand, and it shows.*

### 2. `public/art/scenes/stadium-full-house.webp`: a full stadium on match night
*The same kind of stadium renovated and full at night: bright floodlights, a green pitch, a sea of fans in green and white waving plain flags, a drummer's band in the stands, the players (seen from far away, plain kits) lining up for the anthem. Joyful and loud.*

### 3. `public/art/scenes/stadium-cracked-stand.webp`: a rushed stand that cracked
*During a night match, one section of a newly finished stand has been emptied: stewards in plain hi-vis vests waving fans away from a visible crack in the concrete and a sagging railing, fans crowded at the edge looking anxious, the rest of the stadium still lit and full. Tense, not violent.*

### 4. `public/art/scenes/mini-stadium.webp`: a new community mini-stadium
*A modest new 5,000-seat community stadium in a Nigerian town: a small covered stand on one side, open terraces, a fenced green pitch, and young men and women training (football and athletics on a simple track). Children watching from the fence, a few okadas parked outside. Hopeful, everyday.*

### 5. `public/art/scenes/youth-assembly.webp`: the State Youth Assembly sitting
*A youth parliament in session in a modest hall in a state capital: young Nigerians aged 18 to 30 in a mix of suits, ankara and kaftans at simple desks, a young Speaker in the chair with a plain mace, one member on their feet speaking passionately, others with phones and notebooks. Plain walls, no banners with words.*

### 6. `public/art/characters/football-official.webp`: the visiting football official (portrait)
*A senior official of a continental football body, not a real person: a man in his fifties in a well-cut dark suit, open collar, a lanyard with a blank card, a folder under his arm, a polite but demanding expression. He comes to inspect stadiums and say no.*
