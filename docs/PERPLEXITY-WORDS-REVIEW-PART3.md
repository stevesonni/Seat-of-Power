## The task

You are reviewing the words in **Seat of Power**, a political strategy game played on phones, set in Nigeria. The words below are every line a player saw, in order, during three full playthroughs. Please do two things, **stage by stage**:

1. **Make the words easier to understand.** For each stage, find the words, phrases, numbers and labels a player could misread or not understand. For each one:

2. **Find the gaps and loopholes that cause confusion.** For example:

   - a choice with no downside (an exploit);

   - a governor's word or idea appearing in the presidency.

## Who plays

- Nigerians, mostly young adults, playing on phones in short sessions.
- Many read English as a second language; some play in Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo. The translations are separate, so review the English only.
- Most are not experts in government. They know the big words from the news (FAAC, godfather, Assembly, EFCC) but not always what they mean.
- The game should feel real, and it should teach how Nigerian government works without lecturing.

## How the game works (so you can judge the words)

- **The career:** you create a politician, then run for governor of a state. There are two ways in: a quick start (Route A) or a full seven-step setup (Route B).
- **Governing:** you win, then govern for a 4-year term, and can be re-elected for a second. Each year follows the same order:
  1. the budget (the "budget law" the House of Assembly must pass);
  2. programmes, bills and executive orders;
  3. projects (roads, hospitals …) with contractors;
  4. letters in the mailbox, and event cards;
  5. the end of the year.
- **The four meters on the main screen:** People (approval), Party, Treasury and Godfather. Some screens also show older measures: corruption, security, infrastructure, literacy, health.
- **Tori:** the game's own social network (not Twitter/X).
- **Money:** written in naira. "₦4.2B" means 4.2 billion naira, "₦300M" means 300 million.
- **After the governorship:** the player can run for President (Route C). The presidency runs on the same screens with federal content: ministers instead of commissioners, the National Assembly instead of the House of Assembly, the Federation Account.
- **What is real and what is not:** all people, parties, companies and foreign countries in the game are fictional. Nigerian states, places, laws and institutions are real.

## Rules for the suggestions

- Plain English. Short sentences. Active voice. No jargon unless it is the real Nigerian term players should learn. When a real term stays (for example FAAC), give its plain meaning the first time.
- Keep it Nigerian and real: real states, real laws and real institutions are fine. Do not add real living people, real brands or logos, or real parties.
- Keep the names of fictional people, parties and companies as they are.
- Money stays in naira with B or M (₦4.2B). Suggest when a number needs a comparison, for example "a year of nurses' salaries".
- Prefer showing over telling: a picture, an icon or a number with a direction arrow often beats a sentence.
- Do not rewrite the game's story or events. Only how the words, numbers and labels reach the player.
